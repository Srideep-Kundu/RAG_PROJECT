Correlation vs Covariance

Both covariance and correlation measure the relationship between two
variables, but they differ in interpretation and scale.

Covariance

Covariance indicates whether two variables move together.

-   Positive covariance (>0): Variables increase or decrease together.
-   Negative covariance (<0): One variable increases while the other
    decreases.
-   Zero covariance (≈0): No linear relationship.

Formula

Cov(X,Y) = Σ[(xi-x̄)(yi-ȳ)] / (n-1)

Limitation

Covariance depends on the units of the variables, making its magnitude
difficult to compare across datasets.

Correlation

Correlation is the standardized version of covariance.

Formula

r = Cov(X,Y) / (σxσy)

Range:

-   -1 : Perfect negative correlation
-   0 : No linear correlation
-   +1 : Perfect positive correlation

Interpretation

-   +0.8 to +1.0 : Strong positive relationship
-   +0.5 to +0.8 : Moderate positive relationship
-   0 : No linear relationship
-   -0.5 to -0.8 : Moderate negative relationship
-   -0.8 to -1.0 : Strong negative relationship

Example

Hours Studied and Marks generally have:

-   Positive Covariance
-   High Positive Correlation (close to +1)

Why Correlation is Preferred in Machine Learning

-   Independent of measurement units.
-   Easy to compare different feature pairs.
-   Used in correlation matrices and heatmaps.
-   Helps identify redundant features during feature selection.

Key Differences

-   Covariance shows only the direction of a relationship.
-   Correlation shows both the direction and the strength.
-   Covariance is unbounded.
-   Correlation always lies between -1 and +1.
-   Covariance depends on units.
-   Correlation is unitless.

Interview Tip

The sign of the correlation is always the same as the sign of the
covariance because standard deviations are always positive.
